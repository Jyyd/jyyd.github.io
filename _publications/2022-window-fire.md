Numerical fire simulations investigate how window-opening patterns influence upward fire and smoke spread in a five-storey residential building. The study compares cases with only ground-floor windows open, windows opening as their glass heats, and all windows open. Pressure, temperature, and velocity fields reveal differences in flame propagation and fire spread between floors. Results suggest that increasing turbulence in the lower region under suitable conditions can reduce temperatures higher in the building, offering guidance for facade fire protection and building design.
