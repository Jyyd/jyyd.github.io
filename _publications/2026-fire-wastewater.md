Full-vehicle fire experiments compared the wastewater produced when extinguishing NMC- and LFP-battery electric vehicles. Chemical measurements and cell assays showed that battery chemistry influenced pollutant release and toxicity. Water retained after suppression contained much higher lithium and fluoride concentrations than runoff, and NMC residues were strongly alkaline. Residual wastewater also caused dose-dependent reductions in cell viability. The findings identify contaminated suppression water as an additional accident hazard and support response and wastewater-management practices tailored to battery chemistry.
